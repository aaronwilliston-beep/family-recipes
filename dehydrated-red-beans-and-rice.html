DIY Dehydrated Red Beans and Rice (Backpacking Meal)

Serves 12 trail servings (about 3.1–3.2 oz dry each)Total about 1.5–2 days including dehydrating

Dehydrate

  1. Spread the cooked meal in a single thin layer on the dehydrator trays (his trays hold about 2 servings each). Score the layer with a butter knife to make air channels.
  2. Dehydrate about one night plus one day. After about 12 hours, flip pieces over to free trapped moisture. It's done when everything is crunchy and dry. It loses about half its weight and volume.
  3. Weigh the total dry batch and divide by the number of servings. His batch was 38 oz for 12 servings, about 3.1–3.2 oz each. Freeze while you make the cornbread.
  4. Bake the cornbread thin in a larger pan than usual. Cool, cut into small squares, and dehydrate 3–4 hours.

Pack

  1. Fold a parchment liner and slip it inside each bag. It protects the bag from sharp pieces and doubles as a bowl liner and fire starter at camp.
  2. Weigh a portion into each bag, add 2–3 cornbread squares, and seal.
  3. Store frozen (he's kept them up to 2 years). They keep for weeks unrefrigerated on trips.

Rehydrate in the field

  1. Take the cornbread out. Put the meal bag in an insulated pouch and cover the food with boiling water by about 1/2–1 inch. Close it up.
  2. After about 20 minutes, add the cornbread and a little more water. Wait 10 more minutes.
  3. Eat from the parchment-lined cup or bowl. Mash the cornbread in or eat it separately.

Notes

  • His tips: chop everything fine, especially the meat. Use broth, never plain water, in recipes you'll dehydrate. Season more heavily than usual. Use low-fat meat and rinse it with hot water after cooking, because fat goes rancid.
  • DIY reheater pouch: Reflectix bubble foil cut to about 20 x 16 in, folded and taped to about 9.5 x 13 in, with Velcro closures. It saves fuel compared with simmering.
  • Group option: dump several packs in a pot, cover with water, boil about 10 minutes and rest 10.
