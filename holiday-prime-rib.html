Holiday Prime Rib

Serves about 2 people per rib bone, plus leftoversPrep 15 min (plus 2 hr at room temperature)Cook 5 min per pound at 500°F + 2 hr with the oven offTotal about 4.5–5 hr

Ingredients

Roast

Au jus

Steps

Tap a step to mark where you are.

  1. Take the roast out of the refrigerator and let it sit at room temperature for 2 hours.
  2. Mix the softened butter, pepper and herbes de Provence. Spread it all over the roast.
  3. Salt it heavily on every surface, especially the top. Most of the salt stays on the outside.
  4. Heat the oven to 500°F. Write down the roast's exact weight from the label.
  5. Multiply the weight in pounds by 5, and round up to the next whole minute. That's the high-heat time (example: 5.35 lb × 5 = 26.75, so 27 minutes).
  6. Put the roast in the middle of the oven and roast at 500°F for exactly that many minutes.
  7. When the timer goes off, turn the oven OFF. Do not open the door. Set a timer for exactly 2 hours.
  8. After 2 hours, take it out. It doesn't need to rest; it's been resting in the oven.
  9. Make the au jus while you slice. Serve it boiling hot.
  10. Cut the bones off along the bottom, then slice the roast.

Family history

  • Our holiday roast. We aren't fans of turkey, so it has been the main dish for several Christmases, and also for Thanksgiving and New Year's.
  • We try to get the rib roast from our local butcher, Woody's Butcher Block (https://www.woodysbutcherblock.com/).
  • We love having a lot of leftovers.

Notes

  • The math only works if the oven holds its temperature with the heat off. A well-insulated oven with an accurate thermostat is a must.
  • 2 hours at room temperature is the low end of the time Chef John's written recipe allows. A roast that starts colder can come out a little rarer in the center, so check it before slicing.
  • If you have a wireless probe, or an oven probe whose wire doesn't hold the door open, leave it in the roast. Medium-rare is about 130°F in the center. Don't use a wired probe that keeps the door from sealing, because the method depends on the oven staying closed.
  • If it's too rare after the 2 hours, put it back in a 325°F oven and check every 10 minutes.
  • Warm the plates. Prime rib cools quickly once sliced.
  • Save the bones: brush them with barbecue sauce and roast them until tender.
