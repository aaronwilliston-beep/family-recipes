Thanksgiving Baked Mac and Cheese

Serves 8Prep 25 minCook 30 min (about 15 min stovetop + 12–20 min baking)Total about 55 min

Pasta and sauce

Topping

Steps

Tap a step to mark where you are.

  1. Grease the baking dish or dishes: one 9×13, or several smaller dishes. Shred all the cheese, mix the two kinds, and set 1 cup aside for the top.
  2. Bring a big pot of well-salted water to a boil. Cook the pasta 1–2 minutes less than the package says, because it finishes cooking in the oven. Drain it, but don't rinse it.
  3. While the pasta cooks, melt the butter in a medium pot over medium heat. Whisk in the flour and keep whisking for 1 minute.
  4. Pour in the milk slowly while whisking, so it doesn't lump. Keep whisking until the sauce thickens enough to coat a spoon, about 5–8 minutes.
  5. Take the pot OFF the heat. Stir in the salt, pepper and mustard, then add the cheese a handful at a time, stirring until each handful melts before adding the next. If the cheese stops melting, put the pot back on low for a few seconds, then take it off again.
  6. Stir the drained pasta into the sauce and pour it into the dish or dishes. Top with the saved cheese. Mix the panko with the melted butter and sprinkle it over the cheese.
  7. Bake until it's bubbling at the edges and the top is golden: about 15 minutes at 425°F (or 20 minutes at 375°F) for a 9×13 dish, or 10–15 minutes at 425°F for smaller dishes.
  8. Let it rest about 5 minutes before serving. It will look soupy straight out of the oven, and it thickens as it sits.

Family history

  • The first time: Thanksgiving, November 26, 2026. Dinner was set for noon, and the mac and cheese was the boys' job. Cody shredded the cheddar and Monterey Jack from the blocks, and Carder made the roux and the cheese sauce at the stove. All this happened in a kitchen where the turkey had come out an hour early, the ham had barely warmed in the crockpot and was being rushed through the oven, and the mashed potatoes were being kept warm in a 3D printer.
  • Instead of one 9×13, Carder divided the mac and cheese among three small glass dishes, each topped with the saved cheese and buttered panko. The pasta was a mix of elbows and penne. The dishes went into the 425°F oven next to the ham at about 11:55.
  • When they first checked, it looked soupy, which caused a moment of worry. A few minutes later it was bubbling at the edges and golden on top.
  • It was the hit of the day, judged excellent alongside the ham, and the family decided right away to keep the recipe.

Notes

  • Use block cheese, not pre-shredded. Pre-shredded cheese is coated with starch, which can make the sauce grainy.
  • Always add the cheese with the pot off the heat. Adding it over the burner is the most common way the sauce turns grainy.
  • If the sauce gets too thick, whisk in a splash more milk.
  • Gruyère can stand in for the Monterey Jack for a nuttier, sharper flavor.
  • Leftovers: reheat with a splash of milk or heavy cream to loosen the sauce.
